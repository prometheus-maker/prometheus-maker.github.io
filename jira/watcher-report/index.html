Watcher Report

Find issues nobody watches and issues everyone watches.

Scan a JQL scope for watcher counts, then find unmonitored issues, over-watched ones and issues watched only by deactivated accounts.

What it does

Watchers are the quiet safety net of a Jira site: an issue nobody watches can sit untouched for weeks, and an issue forty people watch sends noise to people who stopped caring. Jira has no page that tells you either.

Watcher Report is an admin page. It scans the issues matching a JQL - by default everything not done - reads each issue's watcher list, and sorts them into three views: no watchers at all, more watchers than a threshold you set between 1 and 100, and issues whose only watchers are deactivated accounts. Rows link straight to the issue.

Two actions follow the report. You can add yourself as a watcher on up to 20 selected issues, and you can remove the deactivated accounts from up to 20 selected issues at once - a clean-up that is otherwise one issue at a time in the watcher dialog.

The scan reads at most 500 issues per run and says so when the scope was cut off. It works through them 40 at a time, continuing on a five-minute schedule, with a progress count on the page. Running a scan needs the Administer Jira permission. Scan results and settings are removed on uninstall.

Install

Where to install it: Jira Cloud, from the Atlassian Marketplace.

Open Apps → Manage your apps in Jira, choose Find new apps, search for the app name and click Install.

What you can do with it

You find it under Jira settings → Apps → Watcher Report.

  • Three views: no watchers, over a threshold, and deactivated watchers only
  • Any JQL as the scope, with a default of everything not done
  • A watcher threshold you set from 1 to 100
  • Add yourself as a watcher on up to 20 selected issues
  • Remove deactivated watchers from up to 20 selected issues at once
  • Scans 40 issues per batch with a progress count, up to 500 per run
  • Admin-only scan; scan data and settings removed on uninstall

Your data

The app runs on Atlassian Forge inside your own site. Settings and any cached results are stored in Forge storage, which belongs to your site; nothing is copied to another service, and nothing is read beyond what the app needs to do its job. Removing the app removes what it stored. Details: privacy policy.
