Checklist Progress for Jira

A checklist field whose progress percentage is searchable in JQL.

Add tickable checklist items to any issue and track completion as a 0 to 100 number you can filter, sort and chart in JQL.

What it does

Acceptance criteria, onboarding steps and definition-of-done lists usually live as bullets in a description, where nothing can count them. You cannot filter for issues that are half done, and a dashboard cannot chart them.

Checklist Progress puts the list in a real Jira custom field. Add items on the issue and tick them as the work completes; the field stores the percentage done and shows a progress bar in the issue view. Items are kept per issue in Forge storage and the field itself writes only the number.

That number is indexed by Jira like any other number field, so JQL finds work in progress and finished issues, saved filters keep a list for a stand-up, and dashboards can chart completion across a project.

Up to 50 items of 200 characters each fit in one checklist. Editing uses the issue's own permissions: if you cannot update the issue, the checklist view tells you so instead of failing silently, and uninstalling the app removes the stored checklists.

Install

Where to install it: Jira Cloud, from the Atlassian Marketplace.

Open Apps → Manage your apps in Jira, choose Find new apps, search for the app name and click Install.

What you can do with it

You find it under the custom field (add it to a screen in Jira settings → Issues → Screens).

  • A checklist editor and viewer in the issue, with a progress bar
  • Completion stored as a number from 0 to 100
  • Filter and chart with plain JQL, no extra query syntax
  • Up to 50 items of 200 characters per issue
  • Respects Jira issue permissions when editing
  • Stored checklists are removed on uninstall

Your data

The app runs on Atlassian Forge inside your own site. Settings and any cached results are stored in Forge storage, which belongs to your site; nothing is copied to another service, and nothing is read beyond what the app needs to do its job. Removing the app removes what it stored. Details: privacy policy.
