Multi-Project Kanban

One Kanban view across projects, straight onto a dashboard.

A dashboard gadget that puts up to 10 projects and a JQL scope into columns by status, with the most recently updated cards first.

What it does

Work splits across projects more often than boards admit it: support bugs in one project, platform work in another, a migration in a third. Jira boards are bound to one project or one filter, so a manager following all three keeps three browser tabs open and still misses the picture.

Multi-Project Kanban is a dashboard gadget. You pick up to 10 projects, add a JQL scope to narrow the work - everything not done by default - and choose how many cards each column shows. The gadget returns one column per status, ordered new, then in progress, then done, with a count on each column and the most recently updated cards beneath it.

Each card shows the issue key, a shortened summary, the issue type and the assignee, so a card with no name under it is visible at a glance. The order is by last update, which means the top of each column is what moved most recently.

One load reads the 100 most recently updated issues that match the scope, so the column counts describe those 100 rather than the whole set; the gadget marks the view when more issues exist than it read. That makes it a stand-up and overview gadget rather than a workload report.

It is read-only and needs no storage: settings live with the gadget, and nothing is written to Jira or kept by the app.

Install

Where to install it: Jira Cloud, from the Atlassian Marketplace.

Open Apps → Manage your apps in Jira, choose Find new apps, search for the app name and click Install.

What you can do with it

  • Up to 10 projects in one gadget
  • A JQL scope to narrow the work, up to 500 characters
  • One column per status, in new, in progress, done order
  • A count per column and 3, 5 or 10 cards shown per column
  • Cards with key, summary, type and assignee, ordered by last update
  • Reads up to 100 issues per load and marks the view when there are more
  • Read-only, with no app storage

Your data

The app runs on Atlassian Forge inside your own site. Settings and any cached results are stored in Forge storage, which belongs to your site; nothing is copied to another service, and nothing is read beyond what the app needs to do its job. Removing the app removes what it stored. Details: privacy policy.
