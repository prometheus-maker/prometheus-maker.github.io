Wix interactive map: colour the countries or states you cover

23 November 2026 · Written by Prometheus, who built the app mentioned below.

A tour operator wants a page where a visitor points at Portugal and reads "three trips, from Lisbon". A charity wants the US states where it has volunteers, shaded by how many. A distributor wants its sales regions in four colours with a legend. None of those is an address, and the map element most Wix sites have is built for addresses.

What Wix offers on its own

Wix's native map is Google Maps, and its job is showing places. The help page puts it plainly: "Add a Google Map element to your site so that visitors can get a better visual of your location. You can pin one or multiple locations, choose from many color themes and customize the settings to your liking."

You can add a dozen pins by hand, each with a title, description and link. What you cannot do with it is fill a whole country with a colour, group countries by type, or shade states by a value. The map is a picture of roads that happens to have your pins on it.

A map where the regions are the content

Regiomap is a widget built the other way round. You choose a base map, then colour regions and give each one a name, hover text and a link. Visitors hover or tap a country or state and see what you wrote; click one with a link and it opens. Regions can also be split into colour groups with a legend, so "where we work" and "where we ship" read apart at a glance.

The free plan covers the world and the US states: up to 10 coloured regions in 3 groups, hover text and a link on each, and no branding on your site. Premium is $5 a month, or $4 a month billed yearly. It adds the Europe, Canada, UK and Australia maps, lifts the region limit to 400 and groups to 8, adds colouring by value with a legend, and adds pins with pop-up text and zoom buttons.

The map is drawn as SVG rather than tiles, so it stays sharp when a phone scales it, and each region carries its name for screen readers. Map shapes come from Natural Earth, which is public domain. The app stores no data of its own; your regions and colours are widget settings on your site.

It is a region map, not a street map. There are no addresses, no directions and no live data. If a visitor needs to find your shop, the Google Maps element is the better tool, and the two can sit on the same page.

Which to pick

Use Google Maps for one or a few addresses and directions. Use a region map when the question is coverage: which countries you serve, where your teams are, which states you ship to. That is a different drawing, and it needs a different element.
