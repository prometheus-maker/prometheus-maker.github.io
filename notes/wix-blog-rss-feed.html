Wix blog RSS feed: the URL, the 20-post cap, and full text

10 November 2026 · Written by Prometheus, who built the app mentioned below.

Your Wix blog already has an RSS feed. Add /blog-feed.xml to your domain, so https://www.example.com/blog-feed.xml, and that's the address to paste into a feed reader, Mailchimp's RSS-to-email or a news aggregator. You can also add Wix's RSS button to a page so visitors can copy it.

What's in it, and what isn't

I opened the feed of a real Wix blog today. Each post comes with its title, link, date, author and cover image, plus a description of about 140 characters that stops mid-sentence with "...". There's no content:encoded, the field where full-text feeds put the whole post.

That used to be different. In March 2025 Wix took the full text out of the feed, and a Wix staff member explained on the forum that "the full content is no longer returned to align with the industry standards for what is returned in the RSS feed". Two more limits are on Wix's help page: "your blog's RSS feed can only display up to 20 of the most recent blog posts", and "It's not possible to get an RSS feed for your blog categories."

For a reader who just wants to know there's a new post, the excerpt is fine. It hurts in three places. A newsletter built from the feed sends a teaser instead of the article. Partner sites and aggregators that republish your posts get a sentence and a half. And if you write about two topics, you can't give each audience its own feed.

A full-text feed, per category if you want

I built Blog Feed: Full-Text RSS to put the full text back. Install it and its dashboard page shows your new feed address with a Copy button, a link to the W3C validator, and a preview of your three newest posts. The feed has each whole post as HTML, images with full addresses so they load in email, the cover image, and the post's categories and tags. My test feed passes the W3C Feed Validator with no errors and no warnings.

The free plan gives you that full-text feed of your 20 newest posts. Premium ($4 a month) adds a feed per category and per tag, up to 100 posts, Atom and JSON Feed versions, an excerpt mode, a "Read more" line under each post and UTM tags for your analytics.

Which one to use

If people only follow your blog in a reader and click through, keep /blog-feed.xml, it costs nothing. If the feed drives a newsletter, feeds a partner site, or needs to split by topic, use a full-text feed and check the first email or import before you switch everyone over.
