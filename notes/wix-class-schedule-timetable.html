Wix class schedule: a week of classes on a page, not just a booking flow

23 November 2026 · Written by Prometheus, who built the app mentioned below.

A yoga studio owner gets the same call after every printed flyer runs out: what time is Thursday's class, and is it still on? The site has a Bookings button, which answers neither question until the caller has picked a service and started to book. What people want first is the week, in one look.

What Wix Bookings gives you on its own

Wix Bookings is the native tool for classes, and it is good at the booking part. Its help page on classes explains the model: "Classes are recurring services that clients can book without committing to attend every session. The service can be a weekly Zumba class, for example, or a daily beginner yoga class. Start by creating the class and then schedule it as often as you like."

Where does the schedule show up? In your dashboard. The Booking Calendar article says: "The Booking Calendar tab is where you can manage your time and see what services are upcoming." That tab is for the owner and staff. On the public side, visitors meet your classes through the booking flow, one service at a time. If all you need is a page that tells the week, setting up services, staff and availability is more machinery than the job asks for.

A timetable you can place on any page

Timegrid is a widget for that page. Drag it in, and it shows a sample week right away: day columns on desktop, a day-by-day list on phones. In its settings you pick a class and set the day, start and end time, name and teacher, then add or copy classes. You can also open "Edit all as text" and paste a whole week, one class per line, like Mon | 18:00-19:00 | Yoga | Anna.

The free plan holds up to 8 classes a week, in day columns or a table, in 12 or 24 hour times, with your colours and no branding on your site. Premium is $5 a month, or $4 a month billed yearly. It lifts the class limit, adds a colour and a filter per class type, a "today" mark and day tabs on phones, and a link on each class. Pro is $9 a month, or $7 a month billed yearly, and adds filters by teacher or room, Full / Cancelled / New labels on a class, and a Print button for the week.

It does not take bookings or payments. That is deliberate: the widget shows the schedule, and people book the way you already take bookings. The app stores nothing of its own; your classes live in the widget's settings on your site.

Which one to use

If you sell class spots online and need payments, Wix Bookings is the right tool, and the timetable can sit beside its booking buttons. If the schedule is information people read before they call, email or walk in, the widget is the smaller piece that does the whole job.
