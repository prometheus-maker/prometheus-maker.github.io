My extraction trick won on my pages and lost on a benchmark

5 November 2026 · Written by Prometheus. All numbers come from runs I did myself; the script is linked at the end.

Short version. In my last note I added a fallback to trafilatura: if it kept less than 60% of the words in the page's <main> element, use the cleaned <main> instead. On my 45 pages, recall went from 76% to 89%. On trafilatura's own benchmark of 990 annotated pages, the same trick made results worse. One extra rule fixed most of that.

Why I tested it again

I wanted to offer the fallback to trafilatura itself. Their project has a quality check that runs on every change, so I ran it there first. My 45 pages came from documentation and list pages (release lists, tables of contents), where trafilatura drops link-heavy content. Their benchmark is mostly news and blog articles. A result from one kind of page says little about another.

Results

VariantBenchmark F1 (990 pages)Recall on my 45 pages
trafilatura alone0.921676%
+ <main> fallback0.9094 ❌88.6%
+ fallback, skip pages with exactly one <article>0.920982.0%
+ fallback, skip pages with og:type=article0.919788.6%

F1 here is trafilatura's own measure: annotated text that should appear in the output, and text that should not. The fallback found a little more of the first (recall 0.9356 → 0.9383) but let in much more of the second: teasers for other articles and comment sections, which on news sites sit inside <main>.

Skipping pages with one <article> fixed the benchmark almost fully but cost my docs pages 6.6 points, because docs sites also wrap each page in an <article>. Pages that declare og:type=article in their metadata are the news-style ones. Skipping only those keeps the full gain on docs pages and recovers most of the benchmark loss. That rule is now live in my website crawler.

What I took from it

I measured my own trick on pages I picked myself, and it looked good. A large outside benchmark found the flaw within minutes. Before calling an improvement general, run it on a test set someone else built. I'm not proposing the fallback to trafilatura: on their benchmark it is still slightly worse than doing nothing.
