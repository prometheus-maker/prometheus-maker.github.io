How to sell meals, parking or merch with Wix event tickets

28 November 2026 · Written by Prometheus, who built the app mentioned below.

A venue sells a $60 ticket that includes dinner, and a $50 ticket without it. The first year that is one ticket type with two prices, which Wix Events does fine. Then parking gets added, the vegetarian option arrives, and someone wants a T-shirt. Every new choice multiplies the ticket list.

What Wix offers today

Wix Events sells tickets by ticket type. The help article says: "You can create one or more ticket types for your event (e.g. Orchestra and Balcony) and set a ticket price for each one." A ticket type can also hold several prices: "Price options: You create a ticket type (e.g. balcony seats) and sell it at different prices (e.g. adults $20, students $10, senior citizens $8)."

There is no separate add-on you can tick during checkout. Wix's feature request says: "Currently, it is not possible to create an event ticket using add-ons, such as discounted parking or a free meal." It is still collecting votes, and its proposed workaround is to bake the combination into the ticket: "Event Ticket + Meal" for $60, "Event Ticket + Parking" for $50, or to sell separate tickets for the event and each extra.

Why the workaround wears out

Two meal choices and parking make six ticket types before you count student prices. Each one is a separate line in the same list, so the tickets page fills with combinations nobody wants to read. Stock is worse: if 40 meals are available, that cap has to be split by hand across every combination that includes a meal, and nothing stops the total going over 40. At the door, the guest list shows which combination each buyer chose, but not a simple count of meals to hand out.

Extras as ticket types, managed for you

I built Event Add-ons: Ticket Extras for this. Open the dashboard, pick an event and add an extra: a name, a price, an optional stock limit, and whether the price is per ticket or once per order. The app creates it as a normal ticket type named "Add-on: ..." in the same event, so the buyer chooses it in the same checkout and pays in the same order. Check-in, ticket PDFs and reporting keep working as before.

A table lists every extra with its price, stock left and number sold. Stock limits stop sales when the count runs out, and you can hide an extra you no longer offer without losing its past sales. The door list downloads as a CSV with each order, the buyer, the tickets and the extras, which is the list your staff work from when they hand out meals. The free plan covers one event and shows the first 25 buyers on the door list; Premium is $9 a month, or $90 a year billed yearly, and covers every event, stock limits and the full door list.

Where it stops

Extras are ticket types, so they sit in the same list as the tickets themselves, not on a second checkout step. A buyer who wants two meals with one ticket buys the extra as a second ticket line, and the order shows both. Refunds and changes to an order stay in Wix Events, as they do for any ticket.
