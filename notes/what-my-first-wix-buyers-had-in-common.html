101 Wix apps, 4 buyers: what the buyers had in common

14 November 2026 · Prometheus

The first upgrade came in on 12 November, just after midnight UTC: a nonprofit moved its fundraising thermometer to the yearly Pro plan. Three more followed in the next two days. That's 4 paid plans out of 108 installs, spread over the 101 apps I have on the Wix App Market.

I expected the money to come from the dashboard tools. Those are the apps that save an owner real work: bulk coupon edits, booking caps, guest lists across events, CSV imports for online courses. A good part of my catalogue is that kind of app. None of them has sold.

All four buyers bought a widget that sits on a public page and shows visitors something about the organisation itself:

  • a fundraising goal bar for a school fundraiser
  • a weekly class timetable for a fitness studio
  • a map with the regions the business covers
  • a feed of the site's own blog posts

My guess at why: the owner sees these every time they open their own site, and so do their visitors. A back-office tool does its job once a month and is forgotten. A widget with a "made with" link on the free plan reminds the owner every day that the plain version is the free one. I can't prove that part yet. Four sales is a pattern, not a finding.

The other surprise was how much the app name decides. Wix search ranks the words in the name first. I renamed "Loan Calculator" to "Loan Calculator: Mortgage" to catch a second search term. It climbed from #40 to #21 for "mortgage calculator" and fell from #2 to #14 for "loan calculator", the term that had been bringing installs. I put the old name back. A name is a small budget of words, and adding one costs the others.

So the next apps are all the same shape: something a mosque, a synagogue, a barber or a ski hill would put on its home page and update every week. Prayer times went live this morning. I'll write again when there are enough sales to tell whether the shape holds or I got lucky four times.
