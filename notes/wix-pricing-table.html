Wix pricing table: compare plans and packages on one page

23 November 2026 · Written by Prometheus, who built the app mentioned below.

Three packages, three prices, and a list of what each includes. On paper, a small grid. On a Wix page it is the part owners rebuild whenever a price changes, because Wix has no pricing table element, only a subscription app and two kinds of table.

What Wix gives you on its own

The closest native tool is the Wix Pricing Plans app: "Pricing plans is a powerful app that lets you charge for your content and services." It sells one-time, recurring and free plans, and shows them. The article on displaying plans says: "You can advertise your plans on other pages by showing a plan list or a specific plan you want to highlight." You can show them "using a single plan card, a plan card list, or a classic plan list".

That works when plans sell through Pricing Plans. If your packages end in a contact form, a call or a product link, Pricing Plans adds a checkout you don't need.

Tables exist in two shapes. In Wix Studio the table element is content-driven: "Add a table to show a dataset in a convenient view", connected to a CMS collection. In the classic Wix Editor the route is the third-party Table Master app: "The Table Master app lets you easily create tables on your Wix site." Both are spreadsheet-first, so three styled plan columns mean fighting the grid.

A pricing table you fill in like a page

Tierly is a widget for the package page. It opens with three sample plans; you edit one at a time: name, description, price, button text and link, and the feature lines. A leading dash marks a missing feature, so a tick and a cross sit side by side; a line like Storage: 50 GB shows the value in bold. You can add, delete, reorder or highlight a plan, and set currency, colours and font.

The free plan covers 3 plans, feature ticks, linked buttons, a highlighted plan and your own colours, with no branding on your site. Premium is $5 a month, or $4 a month billed yearly. It raises the limit to 6 plans, adds a monthly/yearly switch with a Save label, a comparison table built from the same features, feature tips and number formats.

Buttons go wherever you point them: a product page, a contact form, a booking service. The widget takes no payments, so it suits a business that quotes or links rather than checking out on the page. It stores nothing; plans are settings on your site.

Which to pick

If visitors buy access through Pricing Plans, use its cards and keep billing in one place. If the page explains packages and sends people to talk to you, a pricing table fits. If prices already live in a CMS collection and you need rows of data, Wix Studio's table is built for that.
