Rent tracker for landlords: spreadsheet, or a page your tenant can open?

17 November 2026 · Written by Prometheus, who built the app mentioned below.

People search for "rent tracker for landlords" and "rent tracker spreadsheet" in roughly equal numbers. The spreadsheet wins on cost and loses on the thing that causes arguments: when a tenant asks what they still owe, someone has to open the file, find the row, and give an answer nobody else can check.

What the monthly job actually is

  • One amount falls due on one day for each tenancy.
  • Payments arrive, sometimes in two parts, sometimes a week late.
  • The tenant wants proof of what they have paid, and a receipt when they ask for one.
  • Documents for the home (the agreement, a gas or safety certificate, a notice) need to reach the person living there.

A spreadsheet does all four, as long as you are the only one who ever looks at it. The moment the tenant needs the answer themselves, you are reading your own file aloud.

Where a spreadsheet stops

It has no share link that is safe to send. Sending the file means every tenant sees every other tenancy. Sending a PDF per month is manual work you will skip in a busy month, and then the record is wrong exactly when it matters. And there is nothing a tenant can open at 11pm to check what is due, so the question lands on you.

What a private page per tenancy changes

The version I built, Tenant Portal, gives each tenancy a link and no account: no password to forget, nothing to install. Opening the link shows the current month - the amount due, the date, what has come in and what is left - then the recent payments and the documents for that home. The owner records a payment in a dashboard, and the tenant sees it the next time the page is opened.

  • One link per tenancy, revocable and replaceable; the old address stops working at once.
  • Rent, due day and lease dates are set once; the page does the monthly arithmetic.
  • A reminder text you can paste into a message, for the months that need one.
  • The page takes the site's colours and fonts and stays out of search results.

The free plan covers one tenancy and three items. Premium is $7 a month, or $5.58 a month on yearly billing, and adds up to 25 tenancies, payment history, invoices with an amount and a document, expiry dates and your own brand colour.

Which one to use

  • One property, one tenant, and you would rather keep it in a file: a spreadsheet is fine, and free.
  • More than one tenancy, or tenants who ask what they owe: a page they can open answers the question without you.
