Wix typewriter effect: typing text for a headline

23 November 2026 · Written by Prometheus, who built the app mentioned below.

A typewriter headline types itself out, pauses, deletes, then types the next line. It is a common request for a homepage, a bakery banner or a studio landing page. Wix has animations, but typing is not one of them, so getting the effect takes a little care.

What Wix does on its own

The Wix Editor's animation panel offers "entrance, scroll, loop and even mouse effects". The Studio Editor is the same idea: its animations article says "The Studio Editor offers 5 different animation types, based on when you want them to appear." The five are entrance, hover and click, scroll, loop, and WebGL effects for media backgrounds.

All of these move, fade, slide or scale a whole element. None of them types characters into a text box. The old trick is two stacked text boxes with a show and hide animation on each, which tends to flicker and never quite lines up. A GIF or video looks fine, but it hides your words from search engines and screen readers, and you remake the file every time the text changes.

Typing the phrases in a widget

I built Typeline for this. It is a widget you drop on any page. In the settings panel you write the text that stays put, such as "We bake", then the phrases that type, one per line. You pick the font, size, colours, alignment, typing speed, the pause on each phrase, the loop and the cursor. Each change shows in the editor at once.

Three details matter more than they look. The first phrase is real text on the page's first paint, so visitors never watch an empty box and search engines still see the words. The widget keeps the height of your longest phrase, so the content below it does not jump while shorter phrases type. Screen readers get the whole sentence with every phrase, and visitors who ask their device for less motion get a still headline.

The free plan types up to three phrases per widget, with no branding. Premium is $5 a month or $4 a month billed yearly, and adds unlimited phrases, a highlight colour behind the typed words, typing that starts when the widget scrolls into view, random order, and block or underscore cursors.

What it doesn't do

It types one set of phrases in one place on the page. It is not a general animation tool: there is no handwriting or drawing effect, and it does not move other elements. Wix's own entrance and scroll animations are the better fit for those.

Where the effect earns its place

A headline that changes is worth it when you sell several things and one line cannot name them all. Keep it to one spot on a page, use phrases of similar length, and set a pause long enough to read each one. On a phone, a short phrase is kinder than a sentence, because the text shrinks to fit the width.
